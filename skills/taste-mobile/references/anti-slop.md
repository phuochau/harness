# Anti-slop is a product-fit test, not an aesthetic ban list

Avoid design by the average of examples. Defaults often look competent but
anonymous: same pale canvas, same 20px rounded cards, same purple-blue
accent, same pill CTAs, same icon-left statistic cards, same huge heading and
precisely spaced hero, same glass panel in every category.

## Five anti-patterns and better questions

1. **Universal card dashboard:** every piece of content sits in equally sized
   cards. What is the natural form of this product's data/object? A shelf,
   flowing timeline, page, matrix, full-bleed artifact, or direct control may
   tell the story better than cards. Cards are still useful for grouped content.
2. **Unmotivated premium aesthetic:** off-white + bronze + serif for anything
   "luxury". Which specific product materials/rituals create its personality?
3. **Decorative hero area:** half the phone spent on a large greeting and
   decorative illustration. What does the user actually need to do first?
4. **Indiscriminate blur/gradient/shadow:** effects imply hierarchy without
   content meaning. Would a change of scale, layout, imagery or type do better?
5. **Every screen identical:** settings, collections, scanner and editor all
   use the same card shell. Build a consistent *language*, not one frozen page
   template; each screen has a different job.

## Look at these instead

- **Identity:** is the imagery/interaction unmistakably tied to this app?
- **Hierarchical courage:** is one thing obviously more important than others?
- **Composition:** would the screen still make sense in grayscale? What about
  a tiny thumbnail where hierarchy must read immediately?
- **Type craft:** line breaks, numeral alignment, casing, punctuation,
  readability in longer translations and large text.
- **Surface logic:** background, object, control and overlay each have clear
  depth/role rather than arbitrary decorative borders.
- **Emotional range:** delightful for kids can be colorful; deliberate for a
  collector can be quiet; operational utility can be technical. No universal
  "premium" palette exists.
- **Recognition:** put two unrelated app home screens side by side. Are their
  identities meaningfully distinct beyond colors and icons?

## Hard fails for design review

- A style label ("editorial", "luxury", "playful") is the sole rationale.
- Three directions are the same layout with different palettes.
- A concept is based on unavailable assets or fabricated content.
- A key task is hidden to make a screenshot cleaner.
- A scroll layout works as a static image but not when text grows.
- Motion adds confusion, lag or accessibility regression.
- A review claims quality without inspecting real output when output could
  reasonably be inspected.

**Not absolute bans:** gradients, translucency, icons, cards, pills and glass
are valid whenever they serve a user, identity or platform need. Aim for
fit and intent, not aesthetic dogma.
