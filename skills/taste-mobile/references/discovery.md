# Product discovery & mobile reference research

Read this when exploring a new product identity or major redesign. Avoid long
presentations that never lead to a screen.

## Start with the job, not the trend

Write the sentence: **"The user opens this app to _____; success is _____."**
List the two moments worth designing around: initial orientation and completion.
For a camera/scan app the shutter/viewfinder/recognition feedback may be the
entire product's identity; for a collection app the owned objects are its art.

Inventory 8–12 source-specific materials: the output, the thing handled, its
texture, the data's natural shape, the ritual, the cultural context, where the
user uses it, what goes wrong, and what the user is proud to share. Only
include actual features or legitimate planned features. Don't invent scale,
brands, quotes, reviews, numbers or third-party content.

## Benchmark intelligently

Search or inspect **4–8 screens/flows**, where tools permit:

- 2–3 actual category leaders / close competitors.
- 2–3 exceptional adjacent products with relevant interactions (e.g. media
  curation, camera, inventory, workshop, library or journal apps).
- 1–2 deliberate counterexamples to break category-default aesthetics.

Useful starting places, not evidence by themselves:
- https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/
- https://mobbin.com/
- https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/
- https://m3.material.io/
- Relevant live App Store / Google Play product pages and demo videos.

Do not treat awards as a transferable visual style. Extract *why* a specific
pattern suits its user, task and content. If a service requires paid login or
cannot be visited, note that rather than hallucinating contents. Record URLs
and screenshot provenance (page/date, product/version when available).

## Evidence format

For each observed reference, write:

`Name · source URL · observed screen/flow · why it works · what not to copy · relevance to this app`.

Distinguish:
- **Observed**: directly inspected screenshot/video/app in this run.
- **Documented**: supported by accessible published guidelines or descriptions.
- **Hypothesis**: your design inference, unverified by a visual inspection.

Never label a generated mockup "reference app screenshot".

## Concept challenge

Before settling on any direction, fill:

> In this app, **[specific material, object or ritual]** becomes
> **[visual/interaction device]** to help **[specific job]**.

Examples to illustrate *reasoning*, not mandatory styles:
- A perfume collection's bottles become an intentionally lit, browsable archive
  with scan-as-acquisition and ownership states.
- A child's paper clothing drawings become tangible cutouts you move, layer
  and dress on a figure, instead of generic square gallery cards.

**Competitor swap:** replace product name and object with its nearest rival.
If the concept still fits with no modification, it probably expresses a
category default. Sharpen its link to actual material. Do not invent unique
features to pass this test.
